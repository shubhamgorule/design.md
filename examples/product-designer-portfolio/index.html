Collaboration

How I show up in a room

I’m allergic to design-as-decoration. If we’re not changing what someone can do tomorrow, we’re probably polishing the wrong thing.

I draw early, delete often, and send short written notes when they save an hour of meetings. Critique works when we all know which decision is on the table—otherwise it’s just vibes.

I care how things look, but I care more that no one feels stupid using them. Typography and motion are how I say “we thought about you” without saying it out loud.

Outside work: fiction, long walks, side projects that teach me humility. If you’re hiring, skip the polished mission statement—tell me what your team actually fights about on Tuesdays.

Process

How I move work forward

A simple spine—adapt the steps, keep the intent.

  1. Get uncomfortably specific

    Talk to real humans, read the angry tickets, watch a recording if I can. Until we can describe the problem in one honest sentence, we’re not designing—we’re guessing in Figma.

  2. Make the guess cheap

    Maps, flows, ugly screens—whatever forces alignment without mistaking beauty for truth.

  3. Design like someone has to build it Monday

    States, edge cases, empty moments. I sit with engineering early so constraints feel like material, not the enemy.

  4. Stay for the awkward afterparty

    Post-launch is where the ego dies. Watch metrics, fix what we missed, feed it back into the system so the next bet starts smarter.
